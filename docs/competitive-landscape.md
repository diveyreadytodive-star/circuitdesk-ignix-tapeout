## Implication for the demo

The nondeveloper should complete an exact, reproducible task: select a named template, predict its truth table locally, inspect component and monetary cost, publish it through a user-signed transaction on **our** processor, evaluate **our** resulting circuit via read-only `eth_call`, and share proof another browser can check. The panel should see that this helps *make and verify* a circuit. No narrative of agents controlling money or a vault should appear without an actual enforcing integration. A deployed example from another team or from CPU #0 is platform research, not our contest proof.

The contest's seven criteria remain unweighted in the official campaign; this document informs differentiation, not a claimed placement probability.

## Plain judge impact and the one scenario to demonstrate

Remembrance Seal presents a specific Agent-permission story and reports **two** taped-out circuits with public addresses. CircuitDesk's horizontal workbench is easier to reuse across projects but, if shown only as AND/OR/XOR buttons, has a **real application-innovation weakness** next to that entrant. Our advantage must be evidenced in a complete newcomer flow: deterministic template, honest component/fee quote, user-controlled factory path, actual output comparison, and a page another person can reproduce. Until our own processor and circuit are on mainnet, that advantage is only a product design claim, not a contest result.

**Recommended one-minute scenario: “Review disagreement check.”** Two people each mark a release review `Pass` or `Needs work`. The user chooses the XOR template; the local truth table shows `1` only when their flags disagree. After publishing **our** XOR circuit, a second person loads its passport and evaluates the same four input pairs through read-only X Layer calls, comparing the outputs. The shared circuit makes the exact rule independently inspectable without asking reviewers to read a netlist. The app does **not** collect real reviewer identities, trigger a CI action, or prevent a release; those would require separate integrations and authorization.

Why this scenario: a novice can understand XOR and see a clear purpose in four cases; it uses only the supported two-input/one-output circuit semantics A verified on an existing processor; it avoids Remembrance Seal's Agent/wallet/Vault enforcement territory. Its limitation is equally clear: manually supplied flags and read-only results make it a verifiable rule worksheet rather than automated release governance. If the judge expects direct operational impact, the team should acknowledge that as future integration instead of implying it is already delivered.
